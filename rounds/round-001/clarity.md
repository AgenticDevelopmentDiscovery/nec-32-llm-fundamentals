# Clarity & Exposition — Round 1

**Recommendation:** needs revision
The through-line and prose register are sound, but the tutorial's centerpiece section ships with its two figures and its demo replaced by bracketed placeholder text, which is a delivery gap in the shipped register, not a polish issue.

## Strengths

- The WHAT → WHY → HOW → WHAT ELSE arc actually functions as a chain of promises kept: `02-motivation.prose.md` names "context window," "frozen weights," and "hallucination" as surface behaviors, and `03-content.prose.md`'s Self-Attention unit explicitly closes the loop ("the mechanism the 'context window' claims from the motivation section are actually about"). This is the strongest single piece of craft in the document — few tutorials bother to make the callback explicit.
- Jargon discipline is consistently good: "autoregressive," "embedding table," "residual connection," "query/key/value," and "heads" are each defined at first use, in the sentence that uses them, rather than assumed or defined later.
- The prose is genuinely economical — no section pads a slide to fill space, and `01-context.prose.md`'s out-of-scope paragraph does real signposting work (names three deferred topics in one sentence each) without turning into a syllabus.

## Weaknesses

- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — Two of the eight `##` units end in bracketed non-content: "**Figure (not yet embedded):** the encoder-decoder architecture..." and "**Figure (not yet embedded):** the decoder-only architecture...", and the closing unit is "**Demo (not yet built):** load a small open-weights decoder-only model...". Per `topic.md`'s own Scope section, the figures and the demo are not decoration — they're named as the load-bearing, visual/hands-on core of this specific tutorial ("this is a visual tutorial by design"). As currently written, three of the eight slides in the section that is supposed to be the tutorial's center of mass describe what will eventually be there instead of teaching it. This is exactly the pattern CLAUDE.md's own project instructions warn about — a spine promising delivery that the prose hasn't caught up to — except here it's visible in the shipped register itself, not just the sidecar.
- `sections/01-context.prose.md` § "Where Transformers Came From" vs. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § "The Original Transformer: Encoder-Decoder" — both open by restating "Attention Is All You Need" (2017) and the machine-translation motivation, with no cross-reference between them. A reader who just read `01-context` hits the same setup again three sections later with no acknowledgment ("as noted earlier...") that it's a deliberate return to the same fact at higher resolution. Reads as two independent openings rather than one narrative building on itself.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § "From Encoder-Decoder to Decoder-Only" → § "Tokens and Embeddings" — the section pivots from architecture-level comparison (two stacks vs. one) straight into "Before the first layer, every token id is looked up..." with no sentence marking the shift from *which architecture* to *what happens inside it, step by step*. Every other adjacent pair of units in this section signals the handoff in-line (e.g., Self-Attention explicitly reaches back to the context-window claim); this is the one seam without a signpost.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § "Stacking Layers to a Next-Token Distribution" — introduces "repeated N times" without ever grounding N with a number or example (e.g., the demo's own model). A STEM audience will read past it, but it's the one unexplained symbol in an otherwise plain-language walkthrough.

## Actionable

1. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § "The Original Transformer: Encoder-Decoder" and § "From Encoder-Decoder to Decoder-Only" — embed the two figures (or, if genuinely not ready for round 1, replace the bracketed placeholder with a one-line note that reads as intentional, e.g. deferring to a tracked TODO outside the rendered prose) before the next round. (*why:* right now the reader's first encounter with the tutorial's flagship visual is a sentence announcing it isn't there yet — the single largest gap between what `topic.md` promises and what ships.)
2. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § "Demo: A Forward Pass, Layer by Layer" — build the demo or move its description out of the rendered prose entirely (e.g. into the concepts spine as a tracked "not yet done") so the shipped slide doesn't end the tutorial's main section on an admission of incompleteness. (*why:* this is the section's closing beat — ending on "not yet built" undercuts the "what you can do now" landing that `04-conclusion` is about to make.)
3. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § "The Original Transformer: Encoder-Decoder" — add one clause tying back to `01-context`'s "Where Transformers Came From" (e.g., "As introduced earlier, the original Transformer targeted translation; here is the shape that gave it that capability: two stacks...") instead of re-establishing the same fact cold. (*why:* turns a repeated beat into a visible callback, reinforcing the narrative spine instead of reading as duplicated setup.)
4. `sections/03-content.prose.md` — add one bridging sentence at the start of § "Tokens and Embeddings" (e.g., "From here, follow one token through the stack itself, starting with what it looks like before the first layer.") (*why:* every other seam in this section signposts the shift; this is the one place the reader has to infer the change in altitude from architecture-comparison to step-by-step trace.)
