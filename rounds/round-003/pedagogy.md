# Pedagogical Readiness — Round 3

**Recommendation:** minor polish
Delivers on its promise end-to-end with real worked detail; what's left is small, verifiable drift between what the team said it did and what the files actually show.

**Audience read against:** STEM/engineering students who have used or seen agentic AI systems casually and want to understand the mechanism underneath — not ML specialists, but comfortable with vectors, probability, and code.

## Strengths

- The self-attention claim gets a worked example twice, at two levels of trust: the hand-walked "it → cat" query/key/value example in `03-content.prose.md`/`.slidecontent.md`, then the same sentence run through real GPT-2 small in the demo, landing on the same "it → cat" result via an actual attention-weight heatmap. That's the strongest kind of follow-along a tutorial can offer — assert, then let the reader watch the assertion come true on real output — and it's rare to see delivered this cleanly.
- `04-conclusion`'s "What You Can Do Now" reproduces `topic.md`'s "What the reader will be able to do" almost clause for clause. Promise and delivery agree at the level the panel is supposed to check.
- The prose/slidecontent split is holding up under its own stated risk. I checked both registers of every section for drifted claims (topic.md and the sidecars flag this as the thing to watch for now that the two no longer share a source) and found the content itself converged: same claims, same figures, same factual corrections (e.g., the final-layer-norm fix propagated correctly to both `decoder-only.svg`'s caption and `prose.md`'s text). Given how much churn `03-content`'s figures went through this round (per `03-content.concepts.md`'s change log), that's a real achievement, not a given.
- `03-content` is the longest section by a wide margin and gets the most room for the hardest material (self-attention and the residual/normalization mechanics each get their own `##` unit in both registers) — load and pacing follow difficulty, as they should.

## Weaknesses

- `02-motivation.concepts.md` records a specific wordsmithing decision as finished ("Wordsmithed the levers bullet... final: 'these levers make sense instead of feeling arbitrary.'"), but `02-motivation.prose.md` still reads "stop being folklore and start being consequences you can reason about" — the exact phrasing the spine says was abandoned two edits earlier. The sentence itself isn't broken, but the spine is now asserting a delivery that didn't happen, which is precisely the promise/delivery gap this project's registers exist to catch. Costs nothing for the declared reader today, but it means the spine can no longer be trusted at face value as a change log — the next round has no way to tell this apart from an intentional revert.
- `topic.md`'s Demo section promises the `03-content` demo gets "a one-line callback" to sampling temperature, since temperature is "already covered under Why." It isn't there — `temperature` appears zero times in either `03-content.prose.md` or `03-content.slidecontent.md`; the demo talks about sampling from the next-token distribution but never closes the loop back to the concept `02-motivation` spent a paragraph on. A reader who remembers "temperature controls determinism" from `02-motivation` gets no signal that this demo's `torch.softmax` step is that same lever. Minor, but it's a specific thing the proposal said would happen and doesn't.
- The prose/slidecontent instructions for running the demo (`pip install torch transformers matplotlib`, then `python demo/forward_pass.py`) diverge from `demo/forward_pass.py`'s own header, which recommends a venv and a CPU-specific torch wheel (`pip install torch --index-url .../whl/cpu`) precisely to avoid a multi-gigabyte GPU-build download on a machine without CUDA. The tutorial text is the one a reader actually follows — the script's own better advice never made it there. Low-severity (the naive command works, just slower/heavier on some machines), but it's the one place in the tutorial where "follow-along" could visibly stall for part of the declared audience.
