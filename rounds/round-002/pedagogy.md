# Pedagogical Readiness — Round 2

**Recommendation:** minor polish — the architecture walkthrough is coherent, sequenced, and now backed by a real captured demo; two localized fixes (one prerequisite gap, one follow-along gap) are what stand between this and ready-as-is.

**Audience read against:** STEM/engineering students who have heard of or lightly used AI/agentic systems and want to understand the mechanism under the hood — assume general math/CS fluency (vectors, probability, functions), but not ML-specific training vocabulary, since the proposal itself puts training mechanics out of scope.

## Strengths

- The layer-by-layer walkthrough in `03-content` (embeddings → positional info → self-attention → feed-forward/residual/norm → stacking → output distribution) matches the capability `topic.md` and `04-conclusion` promise almost exactly — this is a document that delivers what it said it would, not one that drifted.
- Self-attention gets a real worked example ("The cat sat on the mat because it was tired," tracing *it* → *cat*) instead of staying abstract — exactly the kind of concrete instance the declared audience needs to turn a definition into an intuition.
- The demo is now genuinely worked, not just planned: real GPT-2 small output (tensor shapes per layer, an actual top-5 next-token distribution, a captured attention map) replaces what `topic.md` originally scoped as a description of a plan. This is the single biggest readiness improvement since the last round — the reader sees the mechanism happen on real data instead of taking the walkthrough on faith.

## Weaknesses

- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Feed-Forward, Residuals, and Normalization — the sentence "residual connection adds each block's input back to its output, so information and **gradients** have a direct path through the whole stack... [w]ithout both, stacking more than a handful of layers **stops training reliably**" leans on training vocabulary (gradients, training stability) that the tutorial explicitly scopes out (`topic.md`: "Derivations of the attention math or training algorithms... belong to whichever tutorial covers model training"). A reader who has only lightly used these systems has no supplied definition of "gradient" and is asked to accept a training-dynamics claim the document elsewhere declines to explain. This is unnamed assumed knowledge in the one slide of the walkthrough that most needs to be self-contained, since it is also the densest (three new terms: feed-forward block, residual connection, normalization).
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Demo: A Forward Pass, Layer by Layer — the prose says "Run the same sentence through a real GPT-2 small (`demo/forward_pass.py`)" but never surfaces how. `demo/forward_pass.py` actually has the setup spelled out in its own docstring (venv + `pip install torch`/`transformers`/`matplotlib`, then a run command) — but none of that reaches the rendered document (PDF/slides/site) the declared reader actually gets. Since `topic.md` calls this a "hands-on demo," a reader who wants to follow along rather than just read the captured output has to go spelunking in the repo for instructions the tutorial already has written, just in the wrong file.
- `sections/03-content.concepts.md` / `topic.md` — both still assert figures are "reused as-is, not redrawn" from the source papers, but the shipped figures are hand-drawn SVGs adapted from and attributed to those papers, and the section grew from the spine's confirmed 8 `##` units to 11. This isn't a reader-facing problem — the adapted figures read fine and the attribution is intact — but it means the proposal the panel is supposed to hold the document to no longer describes the document, which is exactly the situation `topic.md`'s own guardrail warns about ("a proposal that no longer matches the tutorial is worse than none").

## Actionable

1. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Feed-Forward, Residuals, and Normalization — replace "so information and gradients have a direct path through the whole stack" and "stops training reliably" with language that doesn't require the reader to already know what a gradient is, e.g. "so information has a direct path through the whole stack, which is what keeps very deep stacks of layers workable" — cut the unexplained training claim rather than gloss it, since training itself is out of scope. (*why:* removes the one clear prerequisite violation in the walkthrough; this alone likely moves the section from minor-polish to ready-as-is.)
2. `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Demo: A Forward Pass, Layer by Layer — add the one-line setup (`pip install torch transformers matplotlib`, then `python demo/forward_pass.py`) already written in the script's docstring, so a reader can act on "hands-on" from the page itself instead of opening the source file to find it. (*why:* closes the follow-along gap on the section's one interactive artifact — cheap fix, direct payoff for the "utilize them more optimally" half of the declared audience.)
3. `topic.md` (Decisions) and `sections/03-content.concepts.md` (Decisions) — update the figure-sourcing decision to say the figures are hand-drawn adaptations, attributed, rather than "reused as-is," and update the confirmed `##` count from 8 to 11. (*why:* lower reader impact, but keeps the proposal usable as the standard the next round's panel holds the document to — an uncorrected proposal here will generate a false "drift" finding later for free.)
