## Weaknesses
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` — the decoder-only figure (`#fig:decoder-only`) is shown once, on its own slide, and then never pointed back to. `topic.md`'s own Shape section says this figure should be "the anchor visual — reused/annotated across multiple `##` units as the walkthrough moves through it," but Self-Attention, Feed-Forward/Residuals/Normalization, and Stacking Layers each teach a piece of what's inside that box without a single "as shown in Figure [decoder-only]" pointer. In the document register this leaves the reader to hold the picture in memory unaided for four subsequent units; on the website it means a reader who lands on "Feed-Forward, Residuals, and Normalization" from search has no link back to the picture that would orient them.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Self-Attention, Visualized — the figure caption ("the query token ('it') compares against every other token's key... dominated by 'cat'") assumes the reader already has the source sentence and the query/key/value vocabulary from the immediately preceding Self-Attention slide. Per the slide-register rule, every `##` unit should stand alone with no memory of the slide before it; a reader who reaches this slide first (website deep link, or flipping the deck out of order) sees "it" and "cat" with no sentence to anchor them.
- `sections/03-content.prose.md` § Feed-Forward, Residuals, and Normalization — no figure at all. This is the one idea in the whole walkthrough that is most inherently relational (a skip connection is, definitionally, an arrow bypassing a box) and the prose has to do all the work: "a residual connection adds each block's input back to its output, so information and gradients have a direct path through the whole stack." That sentence is describing a picture it doesn't have.
- `sections/04-conclusion.prose.md` § Where the Field Is Headed — five distinct developments delivered as one paragraph reads as a disguised bullet list on a projected slide, which works against scannability in both the presentation and website registers.
